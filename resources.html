Q&A

Mental health is an umbrella term for our emotional, psychological, and social well-being. It consists of our thoughts, feelings, and behaviors, affecting how we handle stress, relate to others, and make decisions. Mental health is an extremely crucial part of overall health and is experienced differently from person to person.

Mental health stigma refers to the negative stereotypes, societal disapproval, dehumanization, misconceptions, and shaming that continues to prevail in cultures across the world when discussing mental health.

Stress is the process by which we respond to threatening or challenging events. Anxiety is ongoing worry or fear that continues even when the stressor is gone.

If your feelings are overwhelming, persistent, or significantly interfere with your daily life, it’s a good time to reach out to someone you trust or a mental-health professional.

Therapy generally consists of a private, supportive space where you talk with a trained professional about your thoughts, feelings, experiences, and learn coping skills. (though the format depends largely on the type of therapy).

Many mental health conditions cannot always be prevented or “cured,” especially if it is chronic. However, many can still be managed effectively with support, effective coping strategies, and professional care. Numerous people with severe mental health problems can live normal lives under treatment.

CBT stands for Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy. It is the most common type of therapy used to treat conditions such as depression, anxiety, PTSD, etc. It helps people understand how their thoughts, feelings, and behaviors overlap and teaches skills to manage them.

Mental health issues tend to be more common in women due to a mix of biological, social, and cultural factors, including hormones, stress, and cultural/societal expectations placed on women.

You can talk to your primary care provider, school counselor, or another trusted adult who can help connect you with a mental-health professional.

A trained professional evaluates symptoms, experiences, and history to understand what an individual is going through. The DSM-5(Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders) is often used as a guide to help identify patterns and determine the most accurate diagnosis.
